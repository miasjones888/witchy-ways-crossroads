## I. Continental Freemasonry vs. Anglo-American Freemasonry — The Distinction That Matters

The keyed reader's inheritance is Continental European, not Anglo-American. This distinction is structurally important and is routinely flattened in popular sources.

**Anglo-American mainstream Freemasonry** descends primarily from the United Grand Lodge of England (UGLE, formed 1717), with subsequent transmission to American Masonic Grand Lodges. It is regular Freemasonry — the official, recognized tradition — and is characterized by religious-ecumenical neutrality (members must believe in a Supreme Being, but no particular tradition is specified), male-only membership in mainstream lodges, formal initiation into three Craft degrees (Entered Apprentice, Fellowcraft, Master Mason), and a generally philosophical-ethical orientation rather than an explicitly esoteric one. The various Anglo-American "appendant bodies" (Scottish Rite to the 33rd degree, York Rite, Shrine, etc.) extend the basic three degrees.

**Continental Freemasonry** — sometimes called Liberal Freemasonry, Progressive Universal Freemasonry, or Antient Freemasonry — descends from a different lineage with substantial divergence from the Anglo-American stream. It emerged in France, Italy, Germany, and the Netherlands during the 18th century and was shaped by the Enlightenment, by post-revolutionary French politics, and by the broader Continental esoteric tradition (Hermeticism, Kabbalah, alchemy, Egyptian and Theosophical influence). Continental Freemasonry is characterized by religious freedom (no requirement of belief in a Supreme Being), often mixed-gender or women's lodges, more explicitly esoteric apparatus including the high-degree Egyptian, Templar, and Theurgic rites, and a more politically engaged orientation (Continental Masonic bodies were involved in the French Revolution, the Italian Risorgimento, the secularization movements of late 19th century Europe). It is not recognized by the UGLE as regular Freemasonry, and Anglo-American Masons do not officially visit Continental lodges.

**Why this matters for the keyed reader.** The Dutch ancestor's stream is Continental — almost certainly through the Grand Orient of the Netherlands or one of its esoteric appendant bodies, possibly through the Memphis-Misraim, Strict Observance / Rectified Scottish Rite, or Martinist orders that were active across Continental Europe in the 18th-19th centuries. The relationship to Anglo-American mainstream Freemasonry is one of separate-but-related streams; the relationship to the Anglo-Hermetic Order of the Golden Dawn (1888-1903) is one of substantial overlap, with the Golden Dawn drawing extensively on Continental sources but operating in an English context.

The contemporary practitioner who tries to engage the keyed reader's inheritance through a mainstream Anglo-American Masonic lodge encounters a different tradition than the inherited one. The keyed lineage requires Continental engagement specifically — which is more difficult to access from North America, but which is the actual inheritance.

## II. The Continental European Esoteric Masonic Streams

Three principal streams that were active during the period the keyed reader's Dutch ancestor operated and that are most plausibly the carrier of her specific inheritance.

### Memphis-Misraim — Egyptian Freemasonry

The Rite of Memphis-Misraim was created in Naples in September 1881, emerging from the fusion of two earlier rites: the **Rite of Misraïm** (established in Venice in the late 18th century, brought to France in 1814 by the Bédarride brothers, organized originally in 77 degrees, expanded to 90 by 1811) and the **Rite of Memphis** (founded by Jacques-Étienne Marconis de Nègre in 1838 as a variant of Misraim, combining Templar, chivalric, Egyptian, and alchemical elements, originally 92 degrees).

Memphis-Misraim is commonly called Egyptian Freemasonry due to its extensive use of Hermetic philosophy and Ancient Egyptian symbolism. The system encompasses substantial high-degree material — significantly more degrees than mainstream Anglo-American Masonry — and explicitly draws from Hermetic, Kabbalistic, alchemical, and theurgic sources. The rite is identified within the category "Progressive Universal Freemasonry," "Continental Freemasonry," "Liberal Freemasonry," or "Antient Freemasonry."

Memphis-Misraim was active in Continental Europe through the 19th century and the early 20th, with substantial presence in Italy, France, Belgium, and the Netherlands. It continues today through several successor bodies, with multiple international jurisdictions claiming legitimate descent — the contemporary landscape is fractured, with various "Memphis-Misraim" organizations of varying authenticity.

For the keyed reader, Memphis-Misraim is one of the candidate streams the Dutch ancestor may have been initiated into. Determining specifically which would require genealogical research into the ancestor's documented Masonic affiliations, which may or may not be recoverable.

### The Strict Observance and the Rectified Scottish Rite

The **Rite of Strict Observance** was an occult-infused Masonic order founded in 1754 by Karl Gotthelf von Hund in Germany. It claimed direct descent from the medieval Knights Templar and incorporated Templar mythology, regalia, and ritual into Masonic practice. It expanded rapidly across the Holy Roman Empire and Eastern Europe — from forty lodges in 1768 to over 1,000 across Central Europe at its peak. The system organized lodges into provinces modeled on the medieval Templars, with the first three degrees following standard Masonic structure and the fourth through sixth incorporating Templar lore, including the symbolic apron of Jacques de Molay.

The Strict Observance was dissolved in 1782 after growing dissatisfaction with the failure to deliver promised initiation into the "Mysteries of the Unknown Superiors." Its substantial reform — the **Rectified Scottish Rite** (Rite Écossais Rectifié) — was developed primarily by Jean-Baptiste Willermoz at the Convent of Lyon (1778) and continues today as one of the major Continental European Masonic rites.

The Rectified Scottish Rite is theologically Christian (in distinction from the religious-ecumenical mainstream Anglo-American Freemasonry), preserves substantial Templar symbolism and ritual, and incorporates Martinist mystical material through Willermoz's connection to Saint-Martin and Pasqually. It is active in France, Switzerland, Belgium, Italy, and other Continental European jurisdictions.

For the keyed reader, the Strict Observance / Rectified Scottish Rite is the second candidate stream. Its German-Continental origins and its persistence into the 19th and 20th centuries put it within plausible range of the Dutch ancestor's lifetime.

### Martinism — Christian Mysticism and Esoteric Theurgy

**Martinism** is a form of Christian mysticism and esoteric Christianity centered on the doctrine of the Fall, the materialist condition of humanity, and the process of "Reintegration" (return to the divine source). The tradition descends from three figures whose teachings constitute its three principal currents:

- **Martinez de Pasqually** (c. 1727-1774), founder of the Order of the Élus Cohen (Elect Priests), which practiced Christian theurgy and ceremonial-magical operations.
- **Jean-Baptiste Willermoz** (1730-1824), who organized the Rectified Scottish Rite and preserved the Martinist material in Masonic-knightly form.
- **Louis-Claude de Saint-Martin** (1743-1803), known as "the Unknown Philosopher," whose mystical-philosophical writings developed what is called the "internal Way of the Heart" — an interior, contemplative-mystical path emphasizing direct union with the divine without ceremonial-magical apparatus.

The contemporary **Martinist Order** as an organized initiatic body was reorganized in 1886 by Augustin Chaboseau and Gérard Encausse (Papus). By 1900, under Papus, it had several hundred members across Europe and beyond.

Today, Martinism continues through several lineages: the **Traditional Martinist Order** (rooted in the Western Esoteric Tradition, drawing from Saint-Martin, Pasqually, Willermoz, and Jacob Boehme), the **Martinist Order of the Golden and Rosy+Cross**, the **Ordre Martinistes Souverains**, and various other successor bodies with claims of legitimate descent.

For the keyed reader, Martinism is the third candidate stream. Its Continental European character, its persistence through the 19th and 20th centuries, and its frequent overlap with Memphis-Misraim and Rectified Scottish Rite (many initiates held degrees in multiple bodies) put it within range of the Dutch ancestor's possible affiliations.

## III. Dutch Freemasonry Specifically

The keyed reader's Dutch ancestor would most plausibly have been associated with the Grand Orient of the Netherlands or one of its appendant esoteric bodies.

**Grand Orient of the Netherlands (Grootoosten der Nederlanden).** Founded December 26, 1756, as the umbrella organization for Dutch Freemasonry. The earliest documented Dutch lodge formed in The Hague around 1731 under the Grand Lodge of England; the first regular Dutch lodge, L'Union Royale, was consecrated November 19, 1734. A 1735 ban on Freemasonry was repealed in 1737, after which Freemasonry flourished in the United Provinces. In 1770, the Premier Grand Lodge of England recognized the Grand Orient of the Netherlands' independence.

The Dutch Grand Orient was active throughout the colonial period, and Freemasonry reached the Dutch East Indies (modern Indonesia) as early as 1764 with the founding of La Choisie in Batavia. The Dutch overseas colonies — Suriname, the Dutch Caribbean, the Dutch East Indies — saw substantial Masonic activity during the centuries of Dutch colonial expansion, including at the height of the Atlantic slave trade. The Dutch participation in the Atlantic slave trade (the Dutch West India Company operated from 1621 to 1791, transporting an estimated 550,000 enslaved Africans) intersects directly with the Dutch Masonic establishment of the same period — Dutch slave traders and slaveholders were not infrequently Freemasons, though the historiographical literature on this specific intersection is thin and underdeveloped.

**Higher degrees and esoteric appendant bodies.** The Dutch Grand Orient practices the standard three Craft degrees; higher degrees and esoteric work are practiced through appendant orders that are officially independent but whose members must also belong to a regular Grand Orient lodge. The **Societas Rosicruciana in Anglia** has been active in the Netherlands as a related esoteric body. Other appendant bodies operating in the Continental European jurisdictional context have historically included Dutch implementations of the Memphis-Misraim, Rectified Scottish Rite, and Martinist material.

**Freemasonry Museum in The Hague (Library and Cultural Center).** The Dutch Masonic Library and Museum maintains historical archives, ceremonial objects, manuscripts, and educational resources. For genealogical research into a specific Dutch ancestor's possible Masonic affiliations, this is the appropriate primary research institution.

**The colonial Dutch context specifically.** The keyed reader's ancestor was a Dutch enslaver in the New World. The Dutch Caribbean colonies (Curaçao, Aruba, Bonaire, Sint Maarten, Sint Eustatius, Saba) and Suriname are the most likely geographies, with Suriname being the major Dutch slaveholding colony in the Americas. Dutch Masonic lodges operated in Suriname (Paramaribo) from at least the late 18th century; the Amsterdam-Paramaribo Masonic connection is documented in Dutch Masonic archives. For genealogical research specifically, the Dutch National Archives (Nationaal Archief) in The Hague maintains records of the West India Company and the colonial administration; the question of whether a specific Dutch ancestor's Masonic affiliation is documented depends on the specifics of the genealogical trail.

## IV. Women in Freemasonry — What's Accessible to the Keyed Reader

Mainstream traditional Freemasonry in most jurisdictions is male-only. The keyed reader's options for engagement with Hermetic-Masonic tradition specifically include the women's and mixed-gender Masonic bodies that emerged in the 19th and 20th centuries.

### Co-Masonry / Le Droit Humain

**International Order of Freemasonry for Men and Women, Le Droit Humain** (founded 1893, Paris). The first Co-Masonic Order and the first truly international Masonic Order. Founded by Maria Deraismes (1828-1894), French journalist, feminist, and woman of letters who became the first woman initiated into a Masonic Order according to the same ritual as that practiced for male initiates (her initiation was January 14, 1882, into Lodge Les Libres Penseurs in Le Pecq, west of Paris; the lodge was suspended from its obedience for the act). Together with Georges Martin and other male Freemasons, Deraismes founded La Respectable Loge, Le Droit Humain, Maçonnerie Mixte, on March 14, 1893, initiating sixteen prominent French women.

Today Le Droit Humain has approximately 32,000 members across more than 60 countries on all five continents. The Order admits both men and women on equal terms, operates in the Continental Masonic register (philosophically liberal, ecumenical or non-deistic, esoterically substantive), and is the principal contemporary mixed-gender Masonic body.

**Order of Universal Co-Freemasonry in Great Britain and the British Dependencies** (founded 1902 by Annie Besant and officers of Le Droit Humain). Besant remained head of the Order until her death in 1933. The Theosophical Society's involvement gave this organization a substantial esoteric-Theosophical orientation in addition to the Masonic apparatus.

Co-Masonry is not recognized by the UGLE or by mainstream Anglo-American Masonic bodies.

For the keyed reader, Co-Masonry is the most directly Continental-tradition-consonant accessible option. Le Droit Humain has lodges in the United States (New Orleans has one of the active American Co-Masonic federations) and offers structured initiation into the Continental Masonic stream specifically. This is the major contemporary pathway for women's engagement with Continental Hermetic-Masonic tradition.

### Order of Women Freemasons (UK)

**The Order of Women Freemasons** (founded 1908 as the Honourable Fraternity of Antient Masonry; renamed in 1958). Formed by men and women who seceded from the Co-Masonic movement in disagreement with the Theosophical orientation; their explicit aim was to return to the traditional workings of English Masonry. The Order initially admitted both men and women but became exclusively female in the early 1920s and remains so. The first Grand Master was W. F. Cobb, Rector of St Ethelburga's church, City of London; all subsequent Grand Masters have been women.

Today the Order has approximately 10,000 members in 358 working craft lodges across the British Isles, Australia, Canada, South Africa, Spain, and Zimbabwe. The Order is not recognized by the UGLE but operates as a parallel female Masonic body following English Masonic tradition.

The Order of Women Freemasons works in the **Anglo-American mainstream tradition** (English Masonic ritual, three Craft degrees plus higher-degree appendant bodies), not the Continental stream. For a practitioner whose inheritance is Continental, this is adjacent rather than directly inherited tradition.

### The Honourable Fraternity of Ancient Freemasons (HFAF)

A second UK women's Masonic body, founded as a successor to the original Honourable Fraternity. Smaller than the Order of Women Freemasons but operates in the same Anglo-American tradition.

## V. Western Hermetic / Esoteric Academic Scholarship

The academic study of Western esotericism has matured substantially in the last forty years and provides serious non-initiated engagement with the keyed reader's tradition.

**Antoine Faivre, *Access to Western Esotericism* (SUNY, 1994).** The foundational academic text in the field. Faivre defines Western esotericism through six characteristics — four "intrinsic" (correspondences, living nature, imagination/mediations, transmutation) and two "secondary" (concordance, transmission). Establishes the field as a serious academic territory.

**Wouter Hanegraaff, *Western Esotericism: A Guide for the Perplexed* (Bloomsbury, 2013).** Contemporary synthesis from the major academic figure in the field; Hanegraaff is professor at the University of Amsterdam (relevant given Dutch context) and is the leading living scholar of Western esotericism. His broader catalogue includes *Esotericism and the Academy* (Cambridge, 2012) and the editorship of the *Dictionary of Gnosis and Western Esotericism*.

**Christopher McIntosh, *The Rose Cross and the Age of Reason: Eighteenth-Century Rosicrucianism in Central Europe and Its Relationship to the Enlightenment* (Brill, 1992).** Specifically relevant to the keyed reader's inheritance — focuses on 18th-century Rosicrucian and esoteric-Masonic tradition in Continental Europe (especially Germany), which is the historical period and geography of the inheritance. McIntosh's broader catalogue includes *The Rosicrucians* (1980/1997) and *Eliphas Lévi and the French Occult Revival* (1972).

**Joscelyn Godwin, *The Theosophical Enlightenment* (SUNY, 1994).** The 18th-19th century esoteric milieu out of which Continental Masonic esotericism emerged. Godwin is a major figure in academic study of Western esotericism; his catalogue extends substantially.

**Aries: Journal for the Study of Western Esotericism** (Brill). Academic journal of the field; verify currency. Publishes contemporary scholarship.

**The Esoteric Book Conference** (Seattle, annual). Practitioner-and-scholar gathering with substantial attendance from the academic Western esotericism field and the practitioner-publisher community.

**The Society for the Academic Study of Magic.** Scholarly society for academic engagement with magical and esoteric traditions.

For the keyed reader, the academic literature provides substantial non-initiated engagement with the inheritance and is the appropriate first layer of research. McIntosh's *Rose Cross and the Age of Reason* is the most directly relevant single-volume entry given the geographical-historical specificity.

## VI. The Work of Repair — Sustained Practice for the Keyed Reader

The keyed reader's specific configuration — descended from both the enslaved and (through her father's matrilineal Dutch ancestor) the enslaver, with the Hermetic-Masonic and West African Diaspora streams entering through the same point of violence — calls for the work of repair as sustained ritual practice rather than as therapeutic concept.

### Resmaa Menakem's framework

**Resmaa Menakem, *My Grandmother's Hands: Racialized Trauma and the Pathway to Mending Our Hearts and Bodies* (Central Recovery Press, 2017).** The foundational text. Menakem (MSW, LICSW, SEP) is the leading voice in contemporary somatic engagement with racialized trauma. He developed Cultural Somatics — the use of body and resilience as mechanisms for growth in the face of racialized trauma. His framework has three principal axes:

- **Recognizing how history is held and replayed by the body's survival responses** — the experience of Blackness and trauma; the experience of whiteness and the white body; the experience of police as a body that has its own trauma physiology.
- **Somatic abolitionism** — working with bodies to develop the capacity to discern the historical from the personal, the personal from the intergenerational, the intergenerational from the persistent institutional.
- **Specific somatic practices** — soothing touch on parts of the body, vocalizations (humming, buzzing, singing, chanting), belly breathing, physical movements (rocking), resourcing practices that focus on sensations arising from painful incidents and develop the body's tolerance for processing them.

Menakem's later work *The Quaking of America* (2022) extends the framework into the political dimension. The Cultural Somatics Institute (culturalsomaticsinstitute.com) is the formal training and community body.

For the keyed reader, Menakem's framework applies in both directions: as descendant of the enslaved (Black body experiencing intergenerational racialized trauma) and as descendant of the enslaver (white-body inheritance entering through the Dutch ancestor, even though the practitioner herself is mixed-race). The work is not pick-one-side; the work is reckoning with both as embodied inheritance.

### Daniel Foor's framework for descendants of those who enslaved others

**Daniel Foor, *Ancestral Medicine: Rituals for Personal and Family Healing* (Bear & Co., 2017).** Foor's ancestors of blood are early German and English settler colonists in North America (Ohio/Pennsylvania region). His work specializes in ancestral lineage healing for people with settler colonial ancestry — meaning he has developed methodology specifically for descendants of those who participated in colonial violence. The Ancestral Medicine network now includes nearly 300 trained practitioners globally.

Foor's framework engages enslaver-descendant inheritance specifically. In published interviews, Foor has discussed how someone with settler colonial ancestry can engage healing practices that include deeply bonding with the land in the manner of indigenous peoples who have inhabited it, while doing the ancestral work of reckoning with what one's ancestors did. The four-stage methodology proceeds from establishing relationship with one well ancestor (stage one — appropriate for solo work), through stages two through four (which require Foor-trained practitioner support and are the work of sustained engagement with the broader lineage).

For the keyed reader, Foor's framework applies specifically: the Dutch ancestor whose violence carries the inheritance forward is the kind of ancestor Foor's stages two through four are designed to engage with. The work requires practitioner support; it is not appropriate for solo book-practice.

### Sarah Schulman on the boundaries of repair

**Sarah Schulman, *Conflict Is Not Abuse: Overstating Harm, Community Responsibility, and the Duty of Repair* (Arsenal Pulp Press, 2016).** Schulman's argument complicates the popular contemporary therapeutic frame around interpersonal harm by distinguishing between conflict (which is part of relationship and requires negotiation) and abuse (which is power-over and requires intervention). The framework matters for the keyed reader's repair work because it provides language for what is actually being repaired and what cannot be repaired by individual practitioner work — large-scale institutional and historical violence cannot be repaired by any one person's practice; what can be repaired is the practitioner's own relationship to the inheritance, the pattern of how the inheritance is carried forward in her body and choices, and (over years) the relational repair that sustained practice makes possible within the family system if any of that family system is alive and willing.

### What the work of repair as sustained practice looks like

Drawing on Menakem, Foor, and Schulman, plus the broader frame of v10 § VII on the body as ground:

- **The work begins in the body, not the head.** Menakem-derived somatic practices (orienting, the long exhale, hand on heart, the resourcing practices) are foundational. The body has to be settled enough to bear the weight of the inheritance work without collapsing.
- **The work distinguishes the historical from the personal.** What the keyed reader is reckoning with is what her ancestors did and what was done to her ancestors — not her own personal moral burden. Conflating the two produces either pathological guilt or pathological denial; the work distinguishes them.
- **The work names the violence directly.** The Dutch ancestor raped one of her enslaved ancestors. This is the conception fact. The practice does not soften it, does not allegorize it, does not rationalize it. The practice holds it.
- **The work distinguishes what is repairable from what is not.** What is not repairable: the original violence; the centuries of slavery in the Dutch-Atlantic system; the broader institutional violence the Hermetic-Masonic tradition was implicated in. What is repairable, slowly, over years: the practitioner's own relationship to the inheritance; the way the inheritance is held in the body; the way the Hermetic-Masonic apparatus carries forward in the practitioner's own work — keeping what is useful, refusing what is harmful, transforming what can be transformed.
- **The work proceeds by stages.** Foor's stage one (relationship with one well ancestor — beyond the immediate trauma, who feels bright and resourced) is the appropriate solo entry. Stages two through four require practitioner support and are the work of sustained engagement.
- **The work has practitioner support.** This is not a solo project. The Foor-trained practitioner network, somatic practitioners trained in racialized trauma, ancestor-work practitioners specializing in slavery-and-enslaver inheritance — these are necessary collaborators, not optional.
- **The work is not finite.** It is sustained practice across years. There is no completion. There is sustained relationship to the inheritance that develops over time.
