## The Macro Arc

The six chapters move through the actual lived shape of an evolving practice from before-the-beginning through long-arc decades. The chapters are not equal in length or weight. **Chapter 4 is the longest** because it is the depth chapter — where the territories the practitioner has built foundational literacy in (the feminine divine, sigil and image, tarot, animism deeper, soul-memory, ancestral work) get developed at depth, and where v10's full survey of traditions sits. It is *not* a "lineage convergence" chapter; it is the chapter where the practice that has been built foundationally in chapters 2-3 starts to develop the specific depths that recognition (if it comes) eventually emerges from. **Chapters 1 and 6 are the shortest** — orientation and long view.

The structural relationships:

Chapter 1 (*Before the beginning*) is the orientation. v10 § I is the spine. It establishes the document as wide-entry rather than lineage-keyed; it names the discipline of the early years as breadth-first; it establishes that lineage commitment, if it ever comes, comes by recognition over years (typically three to five) rather than by decision. It begins the reading log. Mia's specific lineage configuration is named as the ancestral dimension her practice will run alongside — not as the architecture of the practice itself.

Chapter 2 (*The first weeks*) is apparatus-building. v10 §§ II–VI are the spine. The grimoire, altar, ritual basics, the wheel of the year in introductory form, the practice rhythm scaled by capacity, plus regulation-only portions of § VII (body as ground). The reader emerges with a working apparatus and an established rhythm. The bioregional San Diego adaptation begins here as a developing thread — v10 § V already names the question; the new doc develops it.

Chapter 3 (*The first year*) is the apparatus running. v10 §§ V (running), XIII (lunar architecture, Hekatean Deipnon-centered), VII (body deeper, what surfaces), VIII (belief opens — three positions developed substantively), XII (animism early — land sit, plant introduction), and X (historical witch as orienting clarity) are the spine. The first complete turning of the wheel, the first marked lunar cycle, body material surfacing as the apparatus earns the body's trust, the question of belief opening as the practice develops alongside.

Chapter 4 (*The first three years*) is **the depth chapter, not lineage convergence**. v10's middle and most substantial sections — § XI (feminine divine survey, the full landscape), § XIV (sigil and image), § XV (tarot and divination), § XVI (survey of traditions, six tradition-clusters), § XII (animism deeper — familiars, plant-allies, the witchcraft animism specifically), § IX (soul-memory), and § XVII (ancestral and matrilineal work, including the genealogical-research dimension) all live here. Hekate / the chthonic / Sabbatic / cunning-folk are developed at depth as v10 develops them. **The three lineage streams (Welsh, West African Diaspora, Hermetic-Masonic) appear as ancestral dimension woven through the chapter** — Rhiannon as one named figure within the Welsh feminine-divine cluster, the Carolinas Gullah Geechee inheritance as one path in ancestral work, the Continental European Hermetic-Masonic stream as one of the surveyed traditions. They are present and developed but they are not the chapter's keystone.

Chapter 5 (*The deeper years*) is recurring spiral. Years three to ten. v10 §§ XVIII (lineage commitment by recognition) and XIX (finding teachers and community) are the spine, with v10 § VII at excavation depth (with practitioner support). Lineage commitment emerges by recognition — for Hekatean devotion, for the Sabbatic stream, for one of the ancestral streams that has called by name, or remains unsettled in the eclectic-grounded mode v10 names as a legitimate practitioner posture. Teachers and community become possible to find. Body work goes deeper, into excavation territory that requires practitioner support. The work of repair with the violent ancestral inheritance becomes a sustained practice rather than a question.

Chapter 6 (*The long view*) is what sustained practice over decades looks like. Largely new material; v10 doesn't have a closing equivalent. Death practice as the central work of the late practice. Teaching others, when and how. The wheel as one's calendar. The closing return to § I orientation from the other end.

Estimated finished word count: 30,000–40,000. Chapter 4 will run 9,000–12,000. Chapters 1 and 6 will each be 2,500–3,500. Chapters 2, 3, 5 sit between.

## Chapter 1 — Before the Beginning

### Lead premise

The early years of a serious magical and devotional practice are about exploration across traditions and the building of practitioner foundations — not about choosing a lineage. (This is v10 § I's opening, preserved verbatim as the chapter's premise.) Lineage commitment, if it ever comes, is the work of years three, five, ten. The chapter is structured for the wide entry.

### The work the chapter does

It establishes the document as wide-entry rather than lineage-keyed. It corrects the male-skewed historiography of the field actively, naming the foundational women writers in their place. It names the cautions: initiation-required traditions and what they are, body work that requires practitioner support, the appropriate scope of solo work. It names Mia's specific lineage configuration honestly — the matrilineal Welsh, the Carolinas Black inheritance via West African enslaved ancestors, the Continental Hermetic-Masonic via the Dutch ancestor and the violence at its origin — as the ancestral dimension her practice will carry alongside, not as the practice's architecture. It begins the reading log. Nothing has been built yet.

## Chapter 2 — The First Weeks

### Lead premise

Before the reading widens and the traditions arrive, set up the instrument that will hold the work. (This is v10 § II's opening, preserved as premise.) The first weeks build the apparatus: an altar surface, a candle, a grimoire, an opening in the calendar, the rhythm of three or four sittings a week, the first markings in the wheel of the year, the foundational regulation work that the body needs to hold what the practice will eventually surface. None of it looks like what the reader came here for. The container is built before what it holds.

### The work the chapter does

Practical, material, dense with build directives. The reader leaves with a functioning apparatus and an established rhythm, with literacy in basic ritual structure, with the wheel of the year mapped to the actual San Diego seasonal cycle (the bioregional adaptation begins as developing thread, not as fabricated bibliography), and with the regulation work running underneath as the foundation. The practitioner-support boundary is held strictly — regulation is solo-appropriate; excavation is not yet.

### Territories woven through

The grimoire (v10 § II). Altar work (v10 § III). Ritual structure (v10 § IV). The wheel of the year, introductory form (v10 § V). The practice rhythm, scaled by capacity (v10 § VI). The body as ground, regulation only (v10 § VII).

**Practice** (drawn from v10 §§ III, IV, V, VI, VII):

- *The threshold rite* — the inaugural ritual, performed once at the working altar using the basic three-part rite structure. Approach altar, light candle, three slow breaths, touch floor and heart, speak aloud "this is the beginning of my practice," sit ten breaths, speak aloud "this is begun," touch floor and heart, extinguish candle, write a single sentence in the grimoire.
- *The minimum daily contact* — approach altar, light candle, one breath, pull one tarot card and record it in the grimoire by name and date with one sentence of response, one breath, extinguish. ~60–120 seconds.
- *The longer weekly working* — three-part structure, ~15–30 minutes. Three-card divinatory pull on the week, recorded with brief interpretation and reviewed at week's end. One made-thing offering placed on the altar. One outdoor walk without device, three observed elements recorded on return.
- *The two-pole lunar minimum* — dark moon and full moon, twenty-four marked nights per year, full ritual at the altar each. (This connects forward to chapter 3 § XIII.)
- *The eight seasonal stations* — entry-level practice per v10 § V: at the altar on the day of each station, candle of seasonally-appropriate color, three cards on "what is this turning asking of me," recorded in the grimoire.
- *The somatic anchors* — three regulation moves layered into daily working: orienting (turn head, find five things, name them silently), the long exhale (4 in / 8 out, six rounds), hand on heart (one hand on chest, one on belly, breathe into the lower hand for two minutes). Use orienting and the long exhale as the entry to any ritual; hand on heart whenever overwhelm arrives.
- *The pendulation principle* — Levine's Somatic Experiencing protocol that governs all opening work in the chapters ahead.
- *The re-entry rite* — protocol for return after the practice has been interrupted (which it will be, repeatedly). Identical to the threshold rite. Write the date and the word *back* in the grimoire. There is no streak to break, no progress to lose, no apology owed.

**Build** (drawn from v10 §§ II, III, IV, V, VI, VII):

- *The grimoire* — physical notebook, hardcover, sewn binding, A5 to B5, blank or dot-grid. First page: name, date, statement of intent. Reserved sections from the start: card pulls, dreams and signs, working records, made things, correspondences, translations and fragments, reading notes, ancestral material, devotional records.
- *The working altar* — surface chosen, cleared, minimum elements placed (candle, water vessel, object of personal weight). Sat at, lit, ten breaths, extinguished, dated in the grimoire.
- *The developing ritual library* — a section of the grimoire that accumulates the practitioner's own rites: daily contact, weekly working, dark moon, full moon, the eight stations, occasional rites for specific purposes.
- *The wheel calendar* — all eight stations mapped for the next twelve months, with reminders the day before each. Section in the grimoire for seasonal observances, accumulating year over year. Personal associations developing over years rather than borrowed wholesale.
- *The body-and-practice record* — practitioner shortlist (Dr. Holly Siegrist, Dr. Sasha Faust, IVAT, Open Path Collective, Foor-trained network at ancestralmedicine.org, SE practitioner directory at traumahealing.org); regulation diary; explicit list of what's being held for eventual practitioner support.

**Research thread** (drawn from v10 §§ II, III, IV, V, VII):

- Historical grimoires worth knowing as background: *Key of Solomon*, *Heptameron*, *Picatrix*, *Munich Manual*, *Grimorium Verum*. Joseph Peterson's *Esoteric Archives* (esotericarchives.com) hosts critical editions.
- Cross-cultural literature on household sacred space: Diana L. Eck on Hindu altars, Robert Orsi on American Catholic devotional altars, Kay Turner's *Beautiful Necessity* (1999) on women's altars.
- Ritual studies field beyond Bell and Grimes: Roy Rappaport, Tom Driver, *Material Religion* journal, James Hillman and Marie-Louise von Franz on ritual depth-psychologically.
- Cross-cultural seasonal-ritual cycles: Roman calendar of festivals, Greek religious calendar, Jewish liturgical year, indigenous seasonal-ceremonial cycles, East and Southeast Asian agricultural-ritual calendars; Lecouteux on European folk-magical calendar beyond the eight stations.
- The convergence between somatic trauma practice and ritual: Stephen Jenkinson, Joanna Macy, Sophie Strand's *The Flowering Wand* (2022).

## Chapter 3 — The First Year

### Lead premise

The first year is what the apparatus does once it has been running long enough that the body trusts it. The wheel turns once. The lunar architecture establishes. Body material that has been waiting for a container surfaces. The question of belief opens substantively. The world begins to be inhabited.

### The work the chapter does

It moves the practitioner from apparatus-building to apparatus-running. Time is the chapter's main tool. The first complete turning of the wheel is observed. The lunar architecture is established with the Hekatean Deipnon at the dark moon and full moon work as the two operative poles. The body's response to sustained practice is named honestly — what surfaces, when to push and when to pause, where the practitioner-support boundary actually sits in real practice. The question of belief opens as v10 § VIII develops it — three coherent positions a serious practitioner can hold, with substantial philosophical apparatus. The first animist encounters are developed through the land-sit practice. The historical witch is grounded as orienting clarity — three distinguishable witch-figures, the lineage map, where modern serious practice actually descends from.

### Territories woven through

The wheel running for real (v10 § V — the running-tier portions). The lunar architecture (v10 § XIII). Body deeper, what surfaces (v10 § VII — pendulation in operation). Belief opens (v10 § VIII — three positions: literal devotional polytheism, theurgic-Platonic, depth-archetypal). Animism early (v10 § XII — the land sit, plant introduction). The historical witch (v10 § X — orienting clarity, lineage map).

**Practice**:

- *Lunar observation* — one full cycle observed nightly before any lunar working begins. ~60 seconds per night for 29.5 days. The eye is developed before the practice.
- *The two-pole working* — dark moon and full moon, twenty-four marked nights per year. Dark moon: Hekatean Deipnon, or any chthonic-banishing-purifying working. Full moon: esbat working — divinatory operation, charging of waters and tools, sustained altar contact, oracular work.
- *The seasonal observance, fuller* — chapter 2's minimum extended into substantive ritual at each station. Specific food, walk, offering, ritual template developed for each.
- *The pull inventory* — entry-level soul-memory investigation. Written inventory of every period, place, culture, language, aesthetic, phobia, fascination, pull carried with no clear biographical source. Updated over years. (This connects forward to chapter 4 § IX.)
- *Dream attention* — on waning crescent / balsamic / dark moon nights, write down vivid coherent dreams that present as past-life material before phone, before coffee. (Connects forward to chapter 4 § IX.)
- *The metaphysical journal* — monthly, a paragraph on what the practitioner currently believes. Not what the texts say; what is actually held. Date it. Do not edit. The drift becomes visible over time.
- *The land sit* — one specific place outdoors, weekly, ~30–60 minutes, no phone, no book, no agenda. The same place for at least a year. Establishment of relationship rather than meditation.
- *Plant introduction* — one plant per season, three months each, learned deeply. Begin with European witchcraft-traditional: mugwort (*Artemisia vulgaris*), yarrow (*Achillea millefolium*), vervain (*Verbena officinalis*), rosemary (*Salvia rosmarinus*), rue (*Ruta graveolens*), wormwood (*Artemisia absinthium*).
- *Animal attention* — augury and the question of communication. Significant encounters recorded in the grimoire with date, animal, context, what was happening, what was noticed.
- *Grief tending* — Weller-derived rite, quarterly or as needed. Altar set with water in clear bowl, salt in small dish, candle. Sit. Light. Name what is being grieved. Let grief move if it wants. Dissolve a pinch of salt in the water. Pour onto earth.

**Build**:

- *The lunar log* — dedicated section of the grimoire for lunar tracking. Twelve cycles in the first year. Each entry: phase, weather, what was worked, what arrived after.
- *The lunar calendar* — every dark moon and full moon mapped for the next twelve months. Susan Baylies's calendars or Llewellyn's Witches' Calendar. Reminders the day before each.
- *Your own developing theology* — over years, the metaphysical journal accumulates; a separate reading file for philosophical-theological texts; an ongoing *credo* / *position* / *working theology* document revised every six months or year.
- *Soul-memory record* — pull inventory, dream records, regression notes if pursued, recurring themes file, reading file for periods/places/cultures that recur.
- *The book of the living world* — animist practice section: land sit record, plant allies file, animal encounter record, land-history file (Native Land Digital lookup, indigenous names for places near you, ecological history), foraging and gathering log if applicable.
- *Lineage map for your own clarity* — one-page map: cunning-folk → modern Wicca → contemporary traditional witchcraft → Sabbatic stream → Hekatean devotion. Where you situate yourself, refined over time. Point is not commitment; point is knowing what you're choosing among.
